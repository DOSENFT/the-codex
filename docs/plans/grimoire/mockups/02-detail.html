Your hit sets the target alight. It takes 1d6 Fire damage immediately, and 1d6 Fire again at the start of each of its turns until it succeeds on a Constitution save to put the fire out. Not Concentration.

The Hearth Paladin's signature level 1 spell, and the numbers back the flavor.

Honest math

Guaranteed damage is only 2d6 (the hit plus one tick) versus Divine Smite's 2d8, and Fire is the most commonly resisted damage type in the game. On raw expected damage against an average enemy, Divine Smite wins.

Where Searing Smite actually wins

(a) it is NOT Concentration, so it runs alongside Bless, Compelled Duel, or Shield of Faith — Divine Smite competes for the same slot but Searing Smite competes for nothing else; (b) it scales better, +2d6 per slot level versus Divine Smite's +1d8; (c) against a low-Constitution target — most casters, most Fey — the ongoing tick is real damage every round; (d) it breaks Concentration repeatedly. Every tick at the start of the enemy caster's turn forces another Concentration save. One Searing Smite on an enemy wizard can strip three separate concentration spells over a fight. That is the play.

The stealth-killer

Ongoing fire damage means an enemy cannot hide from you. It is taking damage every round and that damage reveals position in most rulings.

At level 15 this becomes your best smite

Smoldering Smite adds 1d8 Fire that ignores resistance and immunity. Read your subclass text carefully — as written it only triggers on Divine Smite, and it uses the wrong action type. Ask your DM to rule it as "any spell with Smite in its name" and Searing Smite becomes the centerpiece of the build. See the errata file.

Target priority

Enemy spellcasters, Trolls (fire stops their regeneration in most rulings), and anything you expect to run away.

Correction for your app

In 2024 this healing costs NO ACTION. The 2014 version required a Bonus Action each turn; the 2024 version does not — it simply happens when you create the aura and at the start of each of your turns. D&D Beyond's own tooltip is known to still show the stale 2014 wording, so most character tools have this wrong. Getting it right changes the spell from a trap into a strong combat pick.

Why that matters so much

With no action cost, Aura of Vitality is 2d6 of free healing every round while you continue to attack twice AND cast Divine Smite with your Bonus Action. You are healing 7 a round for free. Over ten rounds that is up to 20d6, averaging 70 Hit Points, from one level 3 slot — the same total as the level 6 Heal spell, delivered without ever giving up a turn.

The only real cost is Concentration

It competes with Bless, Crusader's Mantle, Beacon of Hope and Faerie Fire. Rule of thumb: Aura of Vitality when the party is taking steady chip damage and the fight will run long; Bless when the fight will be short and decided by hit rates.

Positioning is the skill

It is a 30-foot Emanation anchored to YOU, so it moves as you move and no action is needed to reposition it. You can heal a downed ally across the room without walking there — compare Lay on Hands, which is Touch. In practice this means you should stand where the largest number of wounded allies fall inside 30 feet, which for a Hearth Paladin is the same place your Aura of Protection wants you anyway.

Out of combat it is still the best healing in your book

Cast it the moment a fight ends and top off the whole party over ten quiet rounds, saving everyone's Hit Point Dice for the next Short Rest.

One creature per turn

You cannot split the 2d6. Spend it on whoever is closest to dropping.

The Hearth fighting style

This is "guard the hearth" as a mechanic — you physically intercept damage aimed at someone standing next to you, every round, for free, with no Concentration and no resource cost. At Proficiency Bonus +3 that averages about 8.5 damage prevented per round. Over a long fight it outperforms any damage style.

How preparing works

Paladin · 2024 rules
The rules, as written

Five of them, and only the third one is a surprise

  1. You prepare a list of level 1+ Paladin spells. The number is set by the Prepared Spells column, not by your slots.
  2. Every prepared spell must be of a level for which you have spell slots.
  3. You may replace ONE prepared spell with another Paladin spell whenever you finish a Long Rest.
  4. Spells granted by a Paladin feature as "always prepared" do NOT count against the Prepared Spells number, but they do count as Paladin spells for you.
  5. Cantrips gained from Blessed Warrior do not count against the Prepared Spells number.

Three things to say yes or no to.

1 · The refusal is a card, not a toast. It stays until dismissed and it names the rule. The alternative is the checkbox simply not responding, which is how most character apps do it and which teaches nothing.

2 · "Swap for one of mine" is a second screen, not built here. It would show your seven, you pick the one to drop, and the new one goes in. Cheap, but it is a screen — say if you'd rather the refusal just closed and let you untick manually.

3 · The teaching card shows itself once, uninvited. First time you open the Prep tab after this ships. After that it lives behind "How preparing works" in the header and never interrupts again. If you'd rather it never appeared uninvited, it doesn't.

Not shown and not planned: the 30 locked spells are listed on the Prep page as unpreparable, with the reason. They are not hidden. That was your instruction and it applies here as much as in the Grimoire.

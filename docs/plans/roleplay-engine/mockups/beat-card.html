A · live, aimed at a new player
the default. one tap on “pull someone in”.
→ Sarah first session live · scene-aware

The move

Do Push a second stick into the fire, then slide it toward her side of the ring — you’re making room, not asking permission.
Say “You’ve got the look of someone who left in a hurry. My mother had it for eleven years.”
Ask “What did you take with you that you didn’t need?”

What it’s for

Sarah has never played before, so the worst thing you can hand her is an open floor. This gives her a small, concrete, impossible-to-fail question with a physical action already done for her. She only has to name one object.

If she bites

Take whatever object she names completely seriously — repeat it back once, then ask who gave it to her. Two questions in, she has a backstory she invented herself, and she’ll defend it for the rest of the campaign.

Where it can go

↗Warmer — Nix admits what he carries and can’t put down. Trade for trade.
↗Darker — the object is something she stole. Don’t flinch; ask who’s still looking for it.
↗Wider — turn it on the circle: “Everyone shows one thing they’d run back into a fire for.”

If it lands flat

Answer your own question out loud, laugh at your answer, and pass the fire to the person on her left. She’s off the hook, nobody noticed, and the campfire keeps going.

The move

Do Stop cleaning your weapon. Set it down where everyone can see you set it down.
Say “Before we sleep. One rule for this fire: nobody lies tonight. Starting with me.”

What it’s for

The single most reliable campfire opener in improv: declare a rule, then be the first to obey it. It gives every player at the table permission and a format, and it costs them nothing to join.

If they bite

Go first and go small — a real, unheroic admission, not a monologue. One sentence. Then look at exactly one person and wait. Silence is the invitation; don’t fill it.

Where it can go

↗Escalate — the second truth has to be bigger than the first. Say so.
↗Complicate — someone lies anyway, and Nix knows. Say nothing. Remember.
↗Bond — two truths rhyme. Name the rhyme out loud; that’s a party forming.

If it lands flat

“…and the rule expires at dawn.” Pick the weapon back up. The scene closes itself and nobody had to fail at anything.
C · “Ambush!”, rebuilt
today this is one line. this is the same tap.
→ the DM reaction · 4 sec live · scene-aware

Use it when

The DM has just described something arriving and the table has gone quiet. You have about four seconds before someone asks for initiative and the roleplay window closes for the night.

The move

Do Step in front of the smallest person near you. Not a decision — a reflex. Say the line while you’re already moving.
Say “Behind me. Now. — Talk to me when it’s over.”
Ask To the DM: “What do I smell before I see them?”

Why the question

Asking the DM for one sense detail mid-ambush is the single highest-leverage move in table roleplay. It costs one second, it makes the DM improvise texture instead of numbers, and the whole table leans in — because now the fight has a smell.

Where it can go

↗Into combat clean — you already have position and a protected ally. Roll.
↗Into a scene — whoever you shielded owes you a look afterward. Collect it.
↗Into a mistake — it isn’t an ambush. Nix drew steel on a friend. Better.

If it lands flat

Stay in the stance one beat too long, then: “…huh.” Let the table laugh. A missed read played straight is a character trait.

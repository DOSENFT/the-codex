01a — Play tab as the screen actually shows it · 390×844 · Nix, level 7

What is above the fold, unscrolled: every number a turn is made of, the conditions state, and all six things he can do — plus the deck and the tab bar, which are pinned. Today the same 844px shows HP, fifteen condition buttons, and no options at all.

Measured, not asserted: dropping prose from the row bought a whole option. With a written summary line the sixth option fell below the fold; two lines per row fits the entire list in 662px of scroll viewport (844 − 130 deck − 52 tab bar).

01b — the same surface unrolled (what the scroll contains)

What moved. The vitals card is one block at the top and holds all five numbers a turn is made of. Save DC, initiative, proficiency and spell attack are new to this surface — initiative and proficiency are absent from src/ entirely today.

Active Conditions is folded and shows its own state in the header, so a folded section still tells you it is clear. It springs open, with an ember edge, the moment a condition is on.

Every option is exactly two lines and neither is ever cut off: name + economy + price, then the mechanics — to-hit, dice, save, range, rider. No prose in the row. Both lines are assembled from canon fields, so there is no sentence to truncate and nothing for a human to author. Measured: the canon's own summaries average 230 characters and 61 of 71 run past 140 — any prose line here would have had to be clamped (today's .slice(0,80) + '…') or hand-written for all 71 spells. The plain-English "what does this do" moves to the detail sheet, one tap away, where it leads (02b).

The affordance is a chevron, not words. "tap for full ▸" cost ~100px and made Hearthbrand's mechanics line wrap to a third line at 390px — breaking the two-line promise on the very first row. The whole row is the tap target; a chevron says so in 8px.

Your Reactions is a new band. Every reaction states WHEN before it states what it does, because "when may I use this" is the question. Hearthfire Cloak carries its errata flag inline rather than in a footnote.

Reactions sit below the turn list on purpose — you read them on somebody else's turn, and the deck's Reaction chip opens them in place without scrolling at all (03c). Putting them above the options pushed every option off the screen; I tried it, and it cost more than it bought.

The three competing menus collapse to one. "Actions Reference" and the top "Action" dropdown both disappear into this ranked list; the deck's chips remain the way to filter it.

# The Field — competitive research for a D&D 5e companion app used *beside* a physical table

Researched 2026-09-11. Every factual claim below carries a URL that was actually fetched.
Anything I could not verify is marked **UNVERIFIED** with a note on what I tried.
Captures live in `../ref/`, manifest in `../ref/SOURCES.md`.

**Standing caveat on community sentiment.** Reddit is blocked to this agent's user agent at
both search and fetch (`reddit.com` returns "not accessible to our user agent"). I did not
read a single Reddit thread and nothing here is sourced from one. Where the brief asked for
r/dndnext and r/DMAcademy sentiment, I substituted the fetchable primary equivalents:
**D&D Beyond's own product forums** and **public app-store reviews**, both of which are
first-party user complaints rather than aggregator summaries. That is a real substitution
and it is labelled everywhere it is used.

# PART A — THE FIELD

## The shape of it, before the details

The five named products are not five competitors for the same job. They are four products
that compete for *online* play plus one that is not a table product at all:

- **D&D Beyond** is the only one of the five that genuinely ships a phone app aimed at a
  player sitting at a table — and that app is a character sheet and a dice roller.
- **Roll20 deleted its companion app in February 2026.**
- **Foundry VTT explicitly does not support mobile devices** and will not run on an iPad.
- **World Anvil** is a worldbuilding wiki; the table is not its job.
- **Everweave** is a solo AI text RPG. It replaces the table rather than sitting beside it.

The strongest actual competitors for the beside-the-table job turned out to be products
nobody lists in a competitive brief: **Fight Club 5th Edition** and **Fifth Edition
Character Sheet**, one-time-purchase, offline, no-account phone apps with thousands of
ratings and scores of 4.7★ and 4.3★ respectively. They are covered below.

## 1. D&D Beyond (Wizards of the Coast)

**What it does best.** It is the official licensed digital rules corpus for 5e, and it
markets itself as "your official digital toolset for Dungeons & Dragons" covering character
builder, rules library, campaign management and the Maps VTT
(https://www.dndbeyond.com/ — fetched 2026-09-11). Its player app is the only
first-party phone experience at this scale: 4.6★ from 130K reviews and 5M+ downloads on
Google Play (https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fandom.playercompanion&hl=en_US&gl=US),
4.7★ from ~91K ratings on the App Store
(https://apps.apple.com/us/app/d-d-beyond-player-tools/id1501810129). Both fetched 2026-09-11.

**What it does NOT do.**

- **In-person / physical table.** The dedicated marketing page for the app
  (https://www.dndbeyond.com/player-app, fetched 2026-09-11) never uses the phrase
  "in person". Its three feature bullets are "Ready to Roll", "Take Your Characters
  Everywhere", "Like Upcasting Legend Lore". The framing is portability, not presence at a
  physical table.
- **Physical dice.** The single most in-person-specific feature request — being able to roll
  real dice and type the number in, and still have the app do the maths and log the roll —
  has sat open on D&D Beyond's own forum since **7 July 2022**, with a staff
  "Thank you, noted." on 8 July 2022 and the most recent user reply in **June 2026**. Four
  years, unimplemented.
  (https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/d-d-beyond-feedback/d-d-beyond-mobile-app-feedback/145658-request-physical-manual-dice-rolls,
  fetched 2026-09-11). This is the clearest possible proof that a digital-first company does
  not design for the dice already on the table.
- **Offline.** The store listing does promise "Offline access to your library — never miss
  your books, even in areas without wifi" (Play listing, above). But offline **characters**
  are a different story and the complaints are on Wizards' own forum: a thread titled
  "Offline access really needs to improve - (mobile)" (posted 2 August 2023) records
  "A number of us in our group have had similar issues with using the app offline, like the
  inability to change spells on prep, add basic items and especially level up characters,"
  with no official reply visible
  (https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/d-d-beyond-feedback/d-d-beyond-mobile-app-feedback/177353-offline-access-really-needs-to-improve-mobile,
  fetched 2026-09-11). A separate bug thread records character sheets becoming permanently
  inaccessible after a failed offline load
  (https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/bugs-support/mobile-beta-testing-bugs-support/121413-ios-app-character-sheets-may-become-inaccessible).
  So: books offline, characters unreliably.
- **Roleplay / voice / improv.** Nothing. On the whole subscribe page
  (https://www.dndbeyond.com/en/subscribe, fetched 2026-09-11) the words "roleplay" and
  "improv" appear exactly once each, and both occurrences are inside the legal trademark
  boilerplate — "The World's Greatest Roleplaying Game … © 2026 Wizards." The word "voice"
  appears zero times. There is no roleplay, voice or improv feature marketed anywhere on
  D&D Beyond's subscription or player-app pages.
- **No-account use.** Not possible. Everything routes through a D&D Beyond account and
  campaign; content sharing is an account-level entitlement (see pricing below).

**What people pay, how much, how often.** Subscription, two tiers, three terms. From the
official pricing article (https://dndbeyond-support.wizards.com/hc/en-us/articles/7747225116820-Subscriptions-Pricing,
fetched 2026-09-11, page states "Updated 1 year ago"):

| Tier | 1 month | 6 months | 1 year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | **$2.99** | **$14.99** | **$25.99** |
| Master | **$5.99** | **$29.99** | **$54.99** |

Taxes apply. The page also states: "If you subscribe for 6 months, you will pay for around
5 months. If you subscribe for a year, you'll pay for around 9 months."
Separately, books are sold à la carte — the Play Store IAP list shows source books at
**$29.99 each** (App Store listing, fetched 2026-09-11). The marketing subscribe page shows
the same tiers as "from $2.99 USD/mo" / "from $5.99 USD/mo", discounted to "$2.17" and
"$4.58" on annual.

**Reception.**
- Google Play: **4.6★, 130K reviews, 5M+ downloads** (fetched 2026-09-11, URL above).
- App Store: **4.7★, ~91K ratings** (fetched 2026-09-11, URL above).
- Trustpilot: **1.8 / 5 from 58 reviews**, 63% one-star
  (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/dndbeyond.com, fetched 2026-09-11). **Read this with
  care** — 58 reviews against 130K store reviews is not a representative sample, it is a
  customer-service complaint channel. The complaints are about billing and support, not the
  product. One reviewer's line captures it: "The functionality of the site for playing D&D
  is wonderful, but the customer service is atrocious."

## 2. Roll20

**What it does best.** It is the low-friction browser VTT — no install, no host machine,
play D&D/Pathfinder/Call of Cthulhu in a tab (https://roll20.net/, fetched 2026-09-11).

**What it does NOT do — and this is the headline finding of the whole brief.**

On **18 February 2026** Roll20 published "We're Retiring the Roll20 Mobile App to Build
Something Better. Here's Why." by Dean Bigbee, Director of Operations
(https://blog.roll20.net/posts/were-retiring-the-roll20-mobile-app-to-build-something-better-heres-why/,
fetched 2026-09-11). Verbatim:

> "The mobile app was focused on delivering Roll20 character sheets to your phone and
> tablet. It has some good aspects, but overall, the current experience sucks compared to
> the standards we've committed to. It doesn't support the new D&D sheet and features, and
> your feedback has been clear that it's not solving your core need. We hear you."

They delisted it from the App Store and Google Play that week. Corroboration: the App Store
URL for Roll20 – Character Sheets (id1541218329) now returns **HTTP 404** when fetched
(attempted 2026-09-11, both `apps.apple.com/us/...` and the bare `/app/id...` form).

The replacement is a **mobile browser** page, roll20.net/characters — captured at 390×844 in
`ref/roll20-characters-mobile-mobile.png`. A browser page is, by construction, not offline
and not no-account.

- **In-person:** the retirement post does not mention in-person play at all. Its stated
  rationale is engineering economics — "only managing one codebase, with one simple deploy,
  instead of wasting developer resources on complex multi-platform launches."
- **Offline:** browser-based; no offline story is offered.
- **Roleplay/voice/improv:** none marketed.
- **No-account:** not possible.

**What people pay.** Subscription: Plus and Pro. I could **not** fetch a current 2026 Roll20
pricing page — `roll20.net/buy-a-plan` 404s, and `app.roll20.net/why-subscribe-to-roll20`,
`wiki.roll20.net/Subscription` and `help.roll20.net` all sit behind a Cloudflare bot wall
that blocked both WebFetch (403) and headless Chromium ("Performing security verification",
Ray ID logged). The most recent **official** figures I could actually fetch are Roll20's own
price-increase announcement effective **1 July 2021**
(https://bloghub.roll20.net/posts/roll20-pricing-increase-announcement/, fetched 2026-09-11):

- Plus: "$4.99 per month to **$5.99 per month**"; annual "$49.99 per year to **$59.99 per year**"
- Pro: "$9.99 to **$10.99 per month**"; annual "$99.99 … to **$109.99 per a year**"

**Treat those as 2021 prices.** Whether they are still current in September 2026 is
**UNVERIFIED** — I could not reach a live Roll20 pricing page.

**Reception.**
- iOS app: **delisted**, store page 404 (above). Third-party trackers report its final rating
  as 1.8★ from ~320 ratings, but I could not fetch a first-party page to confirm that number,
  so treat the exact figure as **UNVERIFIED**; the delisting and Roll20's own "the current
  experience sucks" are first-party and confirmed.
- Trustpilot: **2.6 / 5 from 20 reviews** (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/roll20.net,
  fetched 2026-09-11). Twenty reviews. Statistically meaningless; recorded for completeness.

## 3. Foundry VTT

**What it does best.** Self-hosted, one-time-purchase, deeply moddable VTT. Its own tagline:
"A Self-Hosted & Modern Roleplaying Platform", promising "a one time purchase with no
subscription fees or feature gating" and that you "own your own data"
(https://foundryvtt.com/, fetched 2026-09-11).

**What it does NOT do.** Foundry's own FAQ (https://foundryvtt.com/article/faq/, page states
"Updated March 18, 2026", fetched 2026-09-11) is unusually candid and answers three of our
four gap questions directly:

- **Mobile — verbatim:** "At this time Foundry VTT does not support mobile devices as part of
  the core software. Though we have plans to add support for it at a later time, the
  development focus is on providing the best possible experience for the majority of users.
  There are some add-on modules which add support for android devices." And: "due to
  limitations in the Safari browser and the fact that Apple requires other browsers on iPad
  to use the Safari webkit, **Foundry VTT will not work via iPad at this time.**"
  At a physical table, phones and iPads *are* the device. Foundry is structurally absent.
- **Its purpose is to replace the table, not join it — verbatim:** "Foundry VTT provides the
  full set of digital tools necessary to **emulate** and (and often surpass!) the experience
  of playing a game in-person". That is the entire category's stated posture in one sentence.
- **In-person, partially — verbatim:** asked "Can I use this software to run in-person games
  with one view for the Gamemaster and another shared view for players?" Foundry answers
  "Yes, Foundry Virtual Tabletop works great for this use case," by hosting from the GM's
  machine and opening a browser to broadcast a player view. So Foundry *does* serve one
  in-person job — the shared TV/projector map — but only via a laptop the GM already runs.
  It does nothing for the player's own hands.
- **Offline:** better than the rest. "Aside from the initial license activation, nothing would
  block you from moving forward if the main Foundry web server was offline." But this means
  a self-hosted LAN, not an app in your pocket.
- **Roleplay/voice/improv:** it ships voice/video *chat* (listed among "digital dice, audio,
  voice / video"), which is transmission, not practice. No roleplay-craft features.
- **No-account:** players need no licence and no purchase, but they do connect to the host's
  server via an invite link; the host needs a Foundry account to activate.

**What people pay.** One-time. Verbatim from the FAQ: "Individual licenses for Foundry Virtual
Tabletop are sold for **$50 USD**. Each license allows for hosting a single Foundry Virtual
Tabletop game server for an unlimited number of players (**who do not need to purchase the
software**). There is only one type of license … there are no premium tiers, special
editions, or season passes. Future updates to the core software will be made available (for
free) to all license owners." The purchase page states the price as "**$50.00**" and
"a perpetual license to use the software and have access to all future updates"
(https://foundryvtt.com/purchase/, fetched 2026-09-11).

There is a **secondary, voluntary** revenue line: Foundry's Patreon shows **13,326 members**
with a tier starting at **$3/month** (https://www.patreon.com/foundryvtt, fetched
2026-09-11). The FAQ confirms Patreon "is not required to access any software features."

**Reception.** No app-store presence (it is desktop software), so no store rating exists.
Trustpilot has a page for foundryvtt.com but with **only 2 reviews** (TrustScore 3.8)
(https://www.trustpilot.com/review/foundryvtt.com, fetched 2026-09-11) — useless as a signal,
recorded so nobody re-does the search. A meaningful, quantified reception figure for Foundry
is **UNVERIFIED**: I tried Trustpilot, App Store, Google Play and Steam and none of them
index it. The 13,326 voluntary Patreon members are the best available proxy.

**Bonus finding, relevant to PART C claim 4.** Foundry publishes an **AI Content Policy**
(https://foundryvtt.com/article/ai-policy/, "Updated: March 18, 2026", fetched 2026-09-11):
"Prepared content must be human-made… Improvised content may be AI-generated. Content
generated at runtime in direct response to end-user prompts is permitted… Non-compliant
packages will be archived or deleted." It also runs a **"Zero AI"** package tag for modules
"authored without any use of generative AI to create their user-facing content." This is a
sixth data point for the hobby's AI posture that was not on the brief's list — and note the
shape of it: *prepared* content banned, *improvised, runtime, user-prompted* content
explicitly permitted. That is a policy-shaped hole that a roleplay-practice tool fits through.

## 4. World Anvil

**What it does best.** Worldbuilding and campaign wiki: articles, interactive maps, timelines,
manuscripts, family trees, statblock templates, random generators, and a novel-writing tool —
a place to *build and publish* a setting (https://www.worldanvil.com/pricing, fetched
2026-09-11 via headless Chromium; WebFetch gets 403). It claims "Join 3,500,000+
worldbuilders" on that page.

**What it does NOT do.**
- **In-person / physical table:** not its job and not claimed. Nothing on the pricing page
  addresses a session in progress at a physical table.
- **Offline:** the word "offline" appears **zero times** on the pricing page (I counted in the
  extracted text). It is a hosted web wiki.
- **Roleplay/voice/improv:** none.
- **No-account:** no — the page's primary CTA is "CREATE YOUR ACCOUNT", and the entire value
  proposition (content privacy, subscribers, co-authors) is account-scoped.

**What people pay.** Subscription, billed monthly/yearly/lifetime. Verbatim from the pricing
page (annual view), fetched 2026-09-11:

| Tier | Price |
|---|---|
| Master | **$4.50/mo** "billed annually (excl. VAT)" |
| Grandmaster | **$8.25/mo** "billed annually (excl. VAT)" |
| Sage | **$25.00/mo** "billed annually (excl. VAT)" |

A free tier exists ("Everything on the free tier plus…"), and monthly and **lifetime** toggles
are offered. I did not capture the monthly or lifetime figures — the page defaulted to the
annual view and those are the numbers I can quote honestly. Monthly/lifetime prices:
**UNVERIFIED**.

Note the tier ladder is sized in *co-authors and subscribers* (4/9/20 co-authors,
10/100/1000 subscribers) — it monetises the one person who owns the world, exactly like every
other product here.

**Reception.** Trustpilot: **3.8 / 5 from 258 reviews**
(https://www.trustpilot.com/review/worldanvil.com, fetched 2026-09-11). This is the one
Trustpilot figure in this document with a large enough n to mean something.

## 5. Everweave (Endgame Studio)

**What it is.** Not a companion app. An **AI-Dungeon-Master solo text RPG**. Its own store
copy: "At its heart, Everweave is a next-gen text RPG inspired by the spirit of the tabletop
roleplaying games… An AI Dungeon Master guides the adventure… Play DnD anywhere, anytime!"
(https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.endgame.everweave, fetched 2026-09-11).
Google Play categorises it "Adventure · Interactive story · **Single player** · Fantasy ·
Offline."

**What it does best.** It has achieved real distribution fast — **500K+ downloads** — with an
LLM DM, persistent NPC memory, and d20 checks, and it ships updates almost daily (Play listing
"Updated on Sep 11, 2026"; App Store shows v0.11.11 updated "1 day ago").

**What it does NOT do.**
- **In-person / physical table:** nothing. It is explicitly **single player**. It competes with
  the table, not beside it.
- **Roleplay/voice/improv practice:** it is text-only roleplay *performance*, not practice, and
  there is no voice at all.
- **Offline:** Play Store tags it "Offline" but the product is an LLM service gated by a
  message quota, which cannot be genuinely offline. Reviews describe hitting message limits
  and paying to refill. Treat the "Offline" tag as a store category, not a capability.
- **No-account:** the app collects "Personal info, Financial info and 4 others" per its own
  data-safety disclosure.

**What people pay.** Consumable message packs plus subscription — **$2.99 (100 messages) up to
$54.99 (Vanguard monthly tier)** per the App Store IAP list
(https://apps.apple.com/us/app/everweave/id6747261740, fetched 2026-09-11). This is a
free-to-play token-metering model, not a tabletop-software model, and its users say so in
reviews: "I would never pay for a message limit or time limit" (Play review, 3 Sep 2026).

**Reception — and note the split, it matters.**
- **Google Play: 2.1★ from 1.37K reviews, 500K+ downloads** (fetched 2026-09-11, URL above;
  the in-page ratings breakdown shows 2.2 from 1.34K). See `ref/everweave-playstore.png`.
- **App Store: 4.3★ from 8.5K ratings** (fetched 2026-09-11, URL above).

Half a million downloads and a 2.1★ on the larger store. That is the profile of a product
that has found demand and is failing to hold it — see PART C claim 5.

## 6–8. The competitors nobody names (and one of them is beating everyone at our job)

I went looking for what actually wins the beside-the-table job, and found that the honest
answer is a set of cheap, offline, no-account phone apps.

### Fight Club 5th Edition (Lion's Den) — the strongest direct competitor found
**4.7★, 3.9K ratings**, "Free · In-App Purchases"
(https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fight-club-5th-edition/id901057473, fetched 2026-09-11).
It is a phone character sheet that works without an account and without a connection, and the
paid unlock is a **one-time $2.99**. A top review, verbatim from that page: "You can make one
character that is stored on Fightclub for free, or pay only 2.99$ to be able to make as many
characters of you want! **It's a one time purchase too.** … Honestly, I think that fightclub
is better than say, DNDBEYOND."
**Its gap:** it does not track official content — "FC5 does not update the app with new
subclasses, weapons, magic items, etc from new supplements. You only have the base tools from
the PHB." (same page). It wins on *table ergonomics* and loses on *licensed content*. That is
precisely the trade the licensed incumbents cannot make and we can.

### Fifth Edition Character Sheet (Walter Kammerer)
**4.3K ratings**, one-time ~$2.99 unlock
(https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fifth-edition-character-sheet/id967650851, fetched 2026-09-11).
Reviews are explicit that people *chose* to pay: "I paid for this not because I had to, but
because I wanted to."

### D&D 5e Character Keep (Apps by Fenn)
**4.2★, 2.33K reviews, 100K+ downloads**, Google Play data-safety section reads "**No data
shared with third parties / No data collected**"
(https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.putnoczki.CharacterKeep&hl=en_US, fetched
2026-09-11). Store copy: "The free version lets you level up to level 5, and keep 3
characters, and for the price of a coffee you can have it all." Top review: "No account
creation or data collection. No Internet required so you can play anywhere. The upgrade is
lifelong with no subscription."

**Read those three together.** The no-account, offline, one-time-purchase segment exists, is
loved (4.2–4.7★), has hundreds of thousands of installs, and monetises at about **$3
one-time**. It is the only segment serving the physical table properly, and it is starved of
licensed content and of anything beyond a character sheet.

### Also mapped (adjacent, for completeness)
- **Demiplane / Nexus** — the same "official digital toolset" model for *non-D&D* licensed
  systems (Pathfinder, Vampire, Daggerheart). Subscription "**less than $5.00 a month**", and
  the headline benefit is content sharing "**with up to 24 people at a time**"
  (https://www.demiplane.com/blog/subscription-benefits, dated 24 Nov 2024, fetched 2026-09-11).
- **Owlbear Rodeo** — indie browser VTT. Free / **$3.33 per month** (Fledgling) / **$6.66 per
  month** (Bestling), both "Billed yearly" against $3.99 and $7.99 monthly
  (https://www.owlbear.rodeo/pricing, captured 2026-09-11 — WebFetch 403, headless capture in
  `ref/owlbear-rodeo-pricing.png`). Tier copy is the single cleanest statement of who pays in
  this market: "Perfect for **DMs** with a regular game and group of players" / "Perfect for
  **DMs** running multiple games at a time."
- **LegendKeeper** — indie campaign wiki. Free plan + **Pro $7.50/month** billed annually
  ($9/month monthly) (https://www.legendkeeper.com/pricing/, captured 2026-09-11).
- **The voice/roleplay "market" is not a market.** Searching for roleplay/improv/voice practice
  tools returns only (a) real-time voice *changers* (Voicemod, Dubbing AI, Echo Live) and
  (b) AI NPC *chat* narration (RPGVoices, AIPI). All of them are pitched as add-ons to
  Roll20/Foundry — i.e. built for online play. I found **no product that helps a human at a
  physical table get better at speaking in character.** That absence is the opportunity.

# PART C — THE FIVE CLAIMS

Verdict key: **CONFIRMED** (fetched source says this) · **WRONG** (fetched source contradicts
it — corrected text follows) · **UNVERIFIED** (could not be established; what was tried is
stated). Every URL below was fetched by me on 2026-09-11 and the quoted text is verbatim from
the fetched page.

---

## CLAIM 1 — "Hybrid play — digital tools beside a physical table — is the dominant mode: about 60%, WotC, May 2023."

**Verdict: the NUMBER is CONFIRMED. The MEANING is WRONG.**

Source found: GeekWire's report of Wizards' own May 2023 D&D press summit in Renton, where
Wizards presented internal player-population data.
https://www.geekwire.com/2023/dungeons-dragons-in-2023-wizards-of-the-coast-reveals-planescape-revival-and-more/
(Thomas Wilde, **May 23, 2023**; fetched 2026-09-11; capture `ref/geekwire-dnd-2023.png`.)

The exact sentence:

> "According to Wizards' internal studies of the player population, 60% of D&D players are male,
> 39% are female, and 1% identify otherwise; **60% are 'hybrid' players, who switch between
> playing the game physically or online**; and 58% play D&D on a weekly basis."

So the 60% is real, it is Wizards' own figure, and it is from May 2023. But **"hybrid" in the
source means players who alternate between in-person sessions and online sessions** — it does
*not* mean "uses digital tools beside a physical table." Anyone repeating the claim in our
wording is over-reading Wizards' data. Do not use it that way; it will not survive a check.

**The claim we actually want is available, and from a better source.** D&D Beyond's own product
lead stated it as a design premise in the Aug 2026 roadmap (see Claim 2):

> "most players prefer to play together in person, but still rely on a combination of digital
> and analog tools to do so."

That is first-party, current, unambiguous, and says the thing the 60% stat was being used to
say. **UNVERIFIED:** I could not find a WotC-published percentage for "plays in person while
using a digital tool." I searched for the original WotC/D&D Beyond press-summit deck and for a
primary WotC page carrying the 60% figure; the figure appears only in press coverage of the
summit, not on a fetchable WotC page.

## CLAIM 2 — "D&D Beyond's roadmap (forum post #2223, 18 Aug 2026) promises a player app 'focused on in-person play' for early 2027, and lists no roleplay features."

**Verdict: CONFIRMED, on both halves.**

Source: "Mid-Year Update: D&D Beyond's 2026 Development Roadmap", posted **Aug 18, 2026**.
https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/2223-mid-year-update-d-d-beyonds-2026-development
(fetched 2026-09-11; capture `ref/dndbeyond-roadmap-2223-midyear.png`.)

Verbatim, from the "New Play Experience" section:

> "This first release is coming as soon as possible (**likely early 2027**) and is **focused on
> making in-person play simpler and more approachable**. Later in 2027, it will grow to support
> more characters, more content, and more ways to play."

And the design premise, same section:

## CLAIM 4 — "The tabletop hobby's makers reject generative AI."

**Verdict overall: CONFIRMED for the hobby's institutions and creators. WRONG — importantly
wrong — if extended to the hobby's largest corporate owner.** Hasbro is pushing AI *onto* its
own staff. Reported sub-claim by sub-claim.

### 4A. ENNIE Awards policy — **CONFIRMED, and it is a total ban.**
https://ennie-awards.com/revised-policy-on-generative-ai-usage/ — posted **January 26, 2025**

> "Beginning with the **2025-2026 submission cycle**, the ENNIE Awards will **no longer accept

### 4B. DriveThruRPG policy — **CONFIRMED for text, PARTIALLY WRONG for art.** It is not a blanket ban.
Two first-party pages, both fetched via headless Chromium because `help.drivethrurpg.com`
returns 403 to a plain fetch.

"General Content Guidelines", last updated **March 19, 2026**,
https://help.drivethrurpg.com/hc/en-us/articles/12723312467095-General-Content-Guidelines

> "**Roll20 and DriveThru Marketplaces do not accept any product that include any text generated

> "DriveThru Marketplaces **do not accept any standalone artwork products that utilize
> AI-generated tools** to make content, or included token, map, or art 'packs'."

> "Products on DriveThru Marketplaces **cannot include VTT assets such as Tokens, Maps,
> Handouts, Image packs, or Videos that are the result of AI generation**."

> "**Images created with generative AI can be used on your cover image or in your product PDF
> only.**"

> "DriveThru Marketplaces **require partners to set the AI-generated content filter on all
> products**. If AI tools were used to generate any of the content in your title, you are
> required to indicate this using the **Creation Method** filter."

So: AI *writing* is banned outright; AI *art* is permitted inside a book but banned as a
standalone or VTT-asset product, and must be disclosed. Enforcement teeth exist — "Titles that
do not comply are subject to removal… Repeat offenders may have their publishing permissions

The companion page "Managing AI-Generated Content and DriveThru Policy", updated **May 12,

> "AI-Generated content is set to viewable by default. When logged in you can choose to view, or

And a list of licensed community programs that ban generative AI outright: Onyx Path (Storypath

### 4C. Kickstarter AI disclosure rules — **CONFIRMED.**
https://updates.kickstarter.com/kickstarter-project-guidelines-for-ai-generated-content-and-ai-technology/
(fetched 2026-09-11; capture `ref/kickstarter-ai-guidelines.png`). "The policy – which

This is a disclosure-and-consent regime, not a ban — state it that way.

First-party policy, WotC's own "Generative AI art FAQ",

### Claim 4, summarised
The awards body bans it. The dominant storefront bans AI *writing* outright, bans AI art as a
standalone product, forces disclosure on everything else, and gives every buyer a one-click "hide
all AI" switch. The dominant crowdfunding platform mandates disclosure and training-data consent.
The largest VTT bans AI in *prepared* content, permits it in *runtime improvised* content, and
sells a "Zero AI" badge. Wizards' written art policy rejects it, got caught violating it, and lost
a 17-year artist over it. **And Hasbro is simultaneously mandating AI tool adoption internally,
hard enough that 79 of its own Arena developers voted to unionise partly over it.**

The practical read for us: **"AI in the product" is not neutral in this market — it is a
labelled, filterable, award-disqualifying attribute, and the *storefront* norm is disclosure
plus a human-made default.** The one carve-out that the incumbents' own policies bless is
AI *at runtime, in response to the user, improvised* — which is precisely the roleplay-practice
use, and not the content-generation use. If we ship AI, ship it there, say so plainly, and never
ship AI-generated prepared content.

### The shape of the price band
Two clusters, and almost nothing between them:

- **Subscription cluster: $3–$8 per month.** Six of the subscription products sit in a
  $2.99–$8.25 band. That band is the market's revealed ceiling for "a tool beside my game",

  play-time tool.
- **One-time cluster: $2.99 for phone apps, $50 for the host-side platform.** This is where
  the product closest to *our* job actually lives.

## D2. Do subscriptions sustain in this market? — Mixed, and this is where evidence is thin.

**Evidence that they do, at the top:** D&D Beyond has run a paid subscription for years and
still does; Hasbro's FY2025 Form 10-K describes D&D Beyond as
"the premier digital content platform for DUNGEONS & DRAGONS where fans can access online
versions of official rule books, character sheets and catalogs, adventures, and other digital
tools such as character builders and official D&D content available for purchase"
(https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/46080/000004608026000011/has-20251228.htm, fetched
2026-09-11). World Anvil, LegendKeeper, Owlbear Rodeo and Demiplane all continue to operate
subscription businesses.

**Evidence that they do not, at our end of the market:** the thing that actually does our job —
a character sheet in your hand at a physical table — is **not** a subscription business. It is
a **$2.99 one-time purchase** with a 4.7★ / 3.9K-rating incumbent (Fight Club 5e), a 4.3K-rating
runner-up, and a 100K-download Android equivalent selling a lifetime unlock. Nobody in that
segment has successfully converted to recurring revenue.

**The strongest counter-evidence to the subscription model is Foundry.** It charges **$50 once**,
has no player-side fee at all, and is the platform the hobby's power users chose. Its
own FAQ states the economics plainly:
> "only one person (the host) needs to purchase a Foundry license for their group"
(https://foundryvtt.com/article/faq/, capture `ref/foundryvtt-faq-licensing.png`).

**THIN EVIDENCE — state this honestly.** I could not find *any* fetchable, first-party revenue,
retention, churn, ARPU or subscriber-count disclosure for any indie TTRPG tool. Owlbear Rodeo,
LegendKeeper, Demiplane and World Anvil publish no financials. I searched for developer
interviews, revenue-transparency posts and case studies and found none that were fetchable and
first-party. **We know what these tools charge. We do not know whether any of them makes a
living at it.** Any business case built on "indie TTRPG subscriptions are proven" is built on
nothing I could verify.

## D3. Does spending concentrate in the DM? — **YES. This is the best-evidenced finding in Part D.**

This one has a first-party executive statement behind it, not an inference.

**Wizards of the Coast's own President, Cynthia Williams**, at a **UBS virtual fireside chat in
December 2022**, as reported by CNBC (fetched 2026-09-11, capture
`ref/cnbc-dnd-undermonetized.png`,
https://www.cnbc.com/2023/01/13/hasbro-delays-new-dungeons-dragons-licensing-rules.html):

> "**D&D has never been more popular, and we have really great fans and engagement**," Wizards of
> the Coast President Cynthia Williams told investors in December during a UBS virtual fireside
> chat. "**But the brand is really under-monetized.**"

> "Williams noted that **the vast majority of purchases related to D&D comes from dungeon
> masters** — the game organizers who create the setting and challenges players face — **despite
> the fact that dungeon masters account for only 20% of the game's overall user base.**"

And the strategic conclusion she drew, which is the entire reason the incumbent's roadmap looks
the way it does:
> This investment in digital is a strategy Williams says will allow Wizards of the Coast to
> "**unlock the type of recurrent spending you see in digital games.**"

**Every product in this market is priced as if that is true, and the pricing pages say so in
plain English:**

- **Owlbear Rodeo** names the buyer in its tier copy: "Perfect for **DMs** with a regular game
  and group of players" / "Perfect for **DMs** running multiple games at a time."
  (https://www.owlbear.rodeo/pricing)
- **Foundry VTT**: "only one person (the host) needs to purchase a Foundry license for their
  group." (https://foundryvtt.com/article/faq/)
- **Demiplane**: the headline subscription benefit is sharing content "**with up to 24 people at
  a time**." (https://www.demiplane.com/blog/subscription-benefits)
- **D&D Beyond**: the Master tier exists specifically so one payer's content library is
  shareable into a campaign; that sharing pitch is the Master tier's marketing.
  (https://www.dndbeyond.com/en/subscribe)

Four independent products, four different companies, one identical business model: **one payer
per table, everyone else rides free.**

## D4. What this means for pricing us

Read strictly from the evidence above, not from preference:

1. **A per-player subscription above ~$5/month has no precedent in this market that I could
   verify.** The entire band is $2.99–$8.25 and the incumbent's entry tier is $2.99.
2. **The DM is the payer.** WotC's own president put a number on it: ~20% of users, the vast
   majority of spend. Price to the DM, design so the other four people at the table get value
   without an account — that is the shape every successful product here already has.
3. **The in-person segment specifically has only ever monetised as a one-time purchase**
   ($2.99 phone apps, $50 Foundry). That is the price the market has actually *paid* for our
   job, as opposed to prices it pays for adjacent jobs. A subscription here is not impossible,
   but it would be **new**, and nothing in the fetched record says it has been done.
4. **Hasbro's own read is that D&D is "under-monetized"** and its fix is "recurrent spending you
   see in digital games." That is the incumbent telling us where it is going — toward recurring
   digital revenue — and simultaneously (Claim 2) telling us it will not have a competent
   in-person app until early 2027.
5. **UNVERIFIED and worth knowing:** I found no fetchable evidence on what *players* (as opposed
   to DMs) will pay, on price elasticity, or on conversion rates for any tool in this category.
   Treat any player-side revenue projection as unevidenced.

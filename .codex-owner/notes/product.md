# The product

Written 2026-09-11 from `inventory.md`, `vault.md`, `studio.md`, `field.md` and the safety net.
Nothing here is from memory. Where the evidence is thin I say so.

---

## The one sentence

**Every other D&D tool remembers your character for you. The Codex is the only one that
rehearses you into him — a practice room between sessions, and a six-second instrument at the
table.**

The claim "the only one" is load-bearing, so here is the check. Of the five products the owner
named plus the two that actually hold this job, not one has a roleplay, voice or improv feature:

- D&D Beyond's own August 2026 roadmap runs eight sections — DM Scene Prep, Custom Uploads, DM
  Tools Outside of Maps, Game Platform & Rules Engine, New Play Experience, Revamped Site Search,
  Drops, Looking for Group. The words *roleplay*, *voice*, *improv* and *narration* appear
  nowhere in it (`field.md`, https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/2223-mid-year-update-d-d-beyonds-2026-development).
- Roll20 retired its companion app on 18 Feb 2026 — "the current experience sucks compared to the
  standards we've committed to" (https://blog.roll20.net/posts/were-retiring-the-roll20-mobile-app-to-build-something-better-heres-why/).
- Foundry, officially: "At this time Foundry VTT does not support mobile devices as part of the
  core software" (https://foundryvtt.com/article/faq/).
- World Anvil is a worldbuilding wiki. The table is not its job.
- Everweave replaces the table with a machine DM. 4.3★ on iOS, 2.1★ on Android
  (`field.md` — and see the correction to input 6 below).
- **Fight Club 5e / Fifth Edition Character Sheet** — offline, no account, $2.99 once, 4.7★ and
  4.3★. These are the real incumbent and nobody's brief names them.

None of them practise anything. They all store.

## Who it's for

**The player. Specifically, the player who is fine at the rules and freezes at the roleplay.**

Decided on this evidence, and against my own first instinct:

1. **The app is already a player's instrument and it is good at it.** 48 features work well
   (`inventory.md`). The turn screen composes what you can actually do *this second* from real
   2024 rules — it counts Extra Attack (1 of 2 used), it greys out a second levelled spell
   because of the 2024 one-levelled-spell-per-turn rule, and it enforces the action economy
   rather than decorating it. The safety net drives all fifteen of those flows and they pass.
   That is years of work pointed at one person's turn.
2. **The gap in the market is on the player's side of the table.** D&D Beyond's roadmap promises
   a player app "focused on making in-person play simpler and more approachable", "likely early
   2027", and commits to zero roleplay features (link above). The market leader has stated the
   real job out loud and scheduled itself out of it for roughly a year.
3. **The owner is this player.** The whole app was built for one Changeling Paladin, and the one
   sentence in the brief that has real heat in it is his: *"the cold start stops embarrassing you
   when the DM says your name."* That is a player's problem and it is felt, not theorised.

**The honest counter-evidence, which is strong.** Money in this hobby concentrates in the DM.
Wizards' then-president Cynthia Williams told UBS investors that D&D is "really under-monetized"
and that the vast majority of purchases come from DMs "despite the fact that dungeon masters
account for only 20% of the game's overall user base." Three competitors price to exactly that:
Foundry ("only one person (the host) needs to purchase"), D&D Beyond ("up to 12 players across 5
campaigns can access any book you've purchased"), Roll20 ("feature access for the whole game is
based on the Game Creator's current subscription"). One payer per table, everyone else rides
free (`field.md`).

So building for the player means building for the wallet that historically doesn't open. I am
taking that risk deliberately, for one reason: **that evidence is about buying *content*, not
about buying *improvement*.** Nobody has tested whether a player will pay to get better, because
nobody has offered it. It is the single thinnest plank in this plan and §"How this fails" treats
it as the primary risk, not a footnote.

## The moment someone tells a friend

**The DM turns and says your name, and you are already talking.**

Not a screen. That is the point — nobody tells a friend about a screen. They tell a friend that
*you* were good. The Codex earns that moment twice:

- **Thursday (PREP).** Twenty minutes in the practice room: a scene, an aim, the voice, a beat
  you actually say out loud. The Roleplay engine already does this end-to-end and degrades to an
  offline bank when there is no model behind it (`inventory.md`).
- **Friday (PLAY).** Your turn arrives. The screen already knows what you can do; you spend the
  slot, the mark burns, you are done in six seconds and your head comes back up. The person
  across the table with D&D Beyond open is still scrolling.

The observable, tellable thing is not a feature. It is **that you never looked down for long.**
That obeys the law: the table is the experience, the screen is an accent.

## What people pay for, how much, and why they keep paying

### The recommendation: sell the app once at $29. Do not sell a subscription.

This argues with the brief (input 9 wants recurring sales). The evidence is why.

**One-time is the only model this exact job has ever supported.** Every product that does what
we do charges once: Fight Club 5e at $2.99, Fifth Edition Character Sheet at $2.99, Foundry at
$50 one-time perpetual, Dungeon Alchemist at $44.99 one-time. There is no subscription success
story for a companion-at-the-table tool anywhere in `field.md`.

**The subscription band is $3–$5/month and it is crowded**: D&D Beyond Hero $2.99, Owlbear Rodeo
$3.99, Fantasy Grounds Classic $3.99, Kanka $4.99, Demiplane ~$4.99, D&D Beyond Master $5.99.
Entering there means being the seventh-cheapest thing on a list.

**Donations do not fund tools in this hobby, and the numbers are not close.** From Patreon
(`field.md`): Czepeku, who sell battle *maps*, have **16,554 paid patrons**. Dynamic Dungeons,
animated maps, 3,258. Baileywiki, 1,825 paid out of 18,500 members. **Owlbear Rodeo — an entire
working virtual tabletop — has 33 paid patrons.** Content monetises in this hobby by two to
three orders of magnitude more than tools do. That is the most useful fact in the whole
research file and it decides the model.

**And the price has to survive the anti-Sigil test.** Astral Tabletop died of free-tier storage
costs. Sigil died with Hasbro's money behind it because WotC "couldn't sustain the level of
ongoing support." A subscription is a promise of ongoing cost; the Codex has no backend and no
cost per user, so a subscription would be charging rent for a house we don't heat. One price,
paid once, for software that runs on your phone with the plane in airplane mode, is the only
pricing that matches how the thing is actually built.

### Why they keep paying anyway

**Sell the instrument once. Sell the practice repeatedly.**

The Patreon numbers say content sells. So the recurring line is not a subscription to the app —
it is **practice content**: accent packs, scene banks, a season of drills. Bought when wanted,
owned forever, no server. That is the same shape as the thing with 16,554 patrons, and the
opposite shape from the thing with 33.

Evidence this works for practice specifically is **thin** — no TTRPG product sells roleplay
practice today, so there is no comparable to point at. I am not going to dress that up. It is
why Milestone 8 in the roadmap is a paid-pilot test rather than a launch.

### The arithmetic for "$100/month and growing"

Shown, not asserted, at the $29 one-time price:

```
  $29.00  price
×      4  strangers per month
= $116.00  per month
```

Four sales a month clears the goal. At a 3% store conversion that is ~133 product-page visits a
month, or about 4–5 a day. At 1% it is ~400 visits a month. Both are reachable from a single
well-received forum post; neither requires a Critical Role cast member.

For contrast, the subscription path to the same $100: **20 subscribers at $4.99 who all stay.**
Twenty retained subscribers is harder than four strangers, and it has to be re-earned every
month forever. Same money, much worse odds.

**Revenue to date: $0.** The acquisition memo states neither product has ever taken a payment
(`studio.md`). Users, installs, signups: **none found** — one GitHub star per repo.

## Where the owner's thesis is wrong

**Input 1 — "don't fight D&D Beyond head-on."** Half wrong, and the wrong half matters. D&D
Beyond is not the incumbent for this job; it has publicly scheduled itself out of it until early
2027 and committed to no roleplay at all. The incumbent we actually have to beat is a **$2.99
offline character-sheet app with 4.7 stars**. That is a harder opponent than it sounds, because
it is cheap, fast, trusted and already on the phone. Every milestone comparator in the roadmap
is chosen accordingly.

**Input 2 — "hybrid play is 60%, WotC, May 2023."** The number is real, the meaning is not. WotC's
line is that 60% "switch between playing the game physically or online" — players who *alternate
modes*, not players using digital tools *beside* a table. Do not use it. Use D&D Beyond's own
2026 sentence instead, which says the thing you wanted and is first-party: "most players prefer
to play together in person, but still rely on a combination of digital and analog tools to do so."

**Input 6 — "2.12 stars and falling."** Wrong for all four candidates you'd have meant. D&D Beyond
is 4.6 Android / 4.7 iOS. Demiplane has no app. Sigil never had a store listing. Roll20's app is
delisted. The ~2.1 figure is **Everweave on Google Play only** — and the same product is 4.3 with
8,500 ratings on iOS. Drop the two decimals and always name the platform, or the claim falls over
the first time someone checks it.

**Input 7 — "willingness to pay is bad."** Confirmed, and it is the most dangerous true thing in
your brief. It is why the price is one-time and why we test with money before we build for scale.

**Input 9 — "creators at the level of Critical Role."** No path to that was found in any file, and
nothing in the research suggests one can be planned. Keep it as a hope; do not let a milestone
depend on it. The plan clears $100/month without a single famous person.

**Input 8 — "the marks burn when you cast", undo, and the cold start.** All three are right, and
two are further along than you think. The marks already exist and are already precached for
offline (`vite.config.ts`, the marks exception to the no-precache rule). Undo exists but is wired
only to reactions, not to slot spends — which is exactly backwards, since the slot is the thing
you mis-tap in the dark. Milestone 1 fixes that.

## What this is, stated so it survives the Sigil question

Answered from the code, not from intent:

- **No backend, near-zero cost per user.** There is no server in this repo. Storage is the
  browser's; the service worker is 20 lines of hand-written caching we can read in one sitting
  (`vite.config.ts`). Nothing about it gets more expensive as more people use it.
- **Not a virtual tabletop.** There is no map, no grid, no token, no shared canvas anywhere in
  `src/`. The screen is never the table.
- **Local-first.** The whole app works offline; the marks are precached deliberately so a finding
  aid never needs a network in a basement with no bars.
- **A log nobody can rewrite, including us.** This is the one piece not yet in the Codex — it
  exists in the vault as an append-only edit log where undo is a compensating row, never a
  deletion (`vault.md`). That pattern is the Codex's missing undo primitive and the roadmap
  brings it across.
